Repeated signal

Find key details in a long agreement

Situation: You are preparing for a supplier meeting tomorrow. Your manager expects you to explain when the agreement renews, how much notice is required, and whether there are exceptions. The contract is long, and you are worried that a quick search will miss a condition buried in another clause.

Back to N1

Without AI

  1. Search the document for likely terms.
  2. Read matching clauses and nearby sections.
  3. Copy dates and conditions into meeting notes.

Pain: Exceptions can be easy to miss, and the search-and-check cycle takes time.

With Microsoft Copilot

  1. Open an authorized file in Word, or attach it in Copilot Chat if enabled.
  2. Ask for renewal terms, relevant clauses, uncertainties, and section references where supported.
  3. Ask focused follow-ups, then build a short meeting brief.

Tool: Word Copilot or Copilot Chat; file and citation features vary by tenant.

What the trainee gains

How to ask targeted questions, refine the search, and use AI to surface uncertainty rather than guess.

Good result

A brief with verified dates, clause references, and open questions. Every material detail matches the source.

Human check: Verify exact wording, dates, and exceptions in the agreement. Copilot does not provide legal advice.

Directly mentioned

Build a correspondence register

Situation: At the end of the month, your team needs to show which letters were sent, received, and answered. The documents are already stored in an approved folder, but there is no reliable register. You need to capture dates, sender, recipient, subject, and reference without spending the afternoon copying each field by hand.

Back to N2

Without AI

  1. Open each letter and locate the required fields.
  2. Type the details into a spreadsheet.
  3. Recheck names, dates, and references against the files.

Pain: Repeated data entry is slow and creates transcription errors.

With Microsoft Copilot

  1. Select a small batch of authorized sample letters.
  2. Use an enabled file workflow to extract fields into a table; otherwise process one document at a time in Word.
  3. Bring the reviewed table into Excel and mark missing or uncertain fields.

Tool: Copilot Chat file workflow and Excel, subject to tenant and license support.

What the trainee gains

How to request structured extraction, keep uncertainty visible, and validate AI-generated tables.

Good result

One row per source letter, with a matching row count and no guessed values.

Human check: Verify each row against its source. Use approved files only; do not infer missing dates or names.

Directly mentioned

Find the right source and confirm access

Situation: A colleague asks you for the current onboarding procedure before a new project starts. You remember seeing it in SharePoint, but a team also uses Confluence and several old copies circulate by email. You are unsure which sources Copilot can actually search, or whether the answer would reflect your permissions.

Back to N3

Without AI

  1. Search each location the person can access.
  2. Ask a colleague which copy is current.
  3. Compare version dates and decide what to trust.

Pain: Searching can be duplicated, and an old copy can appear authoritative.

With Microsoft Copilot

  1. Confirm which sources and Copilot connections are approved and enabled.
  2. Test a known SharePoint source the user can access; inspect the returned source where available.
  3. If no connection exists, use an authorized file workflow. For Confluence, use only an approved connector or export.

Tool: Copilot Chat or Microsoft 365 Copilot, only with configured sources or files the user may use.

What the trainee gains

How to identify source coverage, permissions, and the right route when a source is not connected.

Good result

A verified current procedure and source, or a clear statement that no approved Copilot route is available.

Human check: Confirm version and permissions. Copilot cannot grant access or bypass a missing connection.

Directly mentioned

Draft a recurring weekly update

Situation: It is Friday afternoon and a project lead needs to send a weekly update before the steering meeting. The facts are scattered across short notes and status rows, and last week's update is the only template. You know the content, but turning it into a consistent summary keeps taking time away from following up on blockers.

Back to N4

Without AI

  1. Gather notes and status entries.
  2. Reformat content into last week’s structure.
  3. Check owners, dates, and wording before sending.

Pain: Repeated formatting and drafting leave less time for follow-up.

With Microsoft Copilot

  1. Provide approved notes, status rows, and the previous template.
  2. Ask Copilot for a concise draft and to flag missing owners or dates.
  3. Compare the draft to its source entries and revise before publishing.

Tool: Copilot Chat or Word, using supported file workflows and approved content.

What the trainee gains

How to reuse a prompt structure, turn rough material into an update, and catch omissions.

Good result

A review-ready update that follows the template and flags incomplete entries.

Human check: Verify every status, owner, and date. The user decides what is appropriate to publish.

Repeated signal

Find overdue actions in a spreadsheet

Situation: Before the weekly stand-up, your manager asks which actions are overdue and who owns them. The workbook has dates, owners, and status values, but some cells are blank and the date formats are inconsistent. You need a trustworthy answer quickly, not just a plausible-looking chart.

Back to N5

Without AI

  1. Inspect headings and date formats.
  2. Create filters, formulas, or a pivot table.
  3. Check results row by row.

Pain: Unfamiliar formulas and inconsistent data can make the analysis slow or misleading.

With Microsoft Copilot

  1. Open an authorized workbook and confirm the data range.
  2. Ask Copilot in Excel to identify overdue rows and summarize by owner if enabled.
  3. Otherwise ask Copilot Chat for a formula using sanitized column names, apply it, and spot-check rows.

Tool: Copilot in Excel where enabled; Chat can help draft formulas but does not verify results.

What the trainee gains

How to translate a business question into analysis steps and inspect formulas, filters, and assumptions.

Good result

A summary of overdue actions by owner that agrees with a manual spot-check.

Human check: Verify date logic, blanks, filters, and source rows. Generated calculations remain unverified until checked.

Directly mentioned

Turn a long report into a presentation

Situation: A steering committee is meeting tomorrow, and you have been asked to brief managers in five slides. The source report is forty pages long and contains findings, risks, and open decisions. You need to make it concise without leaving out the caveat that changes the recommendation.

Back to N7

Without AI

  1. Read the report and mark key findings.
  2. Choose what to include and arrange the story.
  3. Build slides and verify figures.

Pain: Condensing material without losing context takes time.

With Microsoft Copilot

  1. Use an authorized report in Word or a supported Copilot Chat file workflow.
  2. Ask for the main message, evidence, risks, and open decisions as an outline.
  3. Review the outline, then use PowerPoint Copilot to draft slides if enabled; otherwise create slides from the outline.

Tool: Word, PowerPoint, or Copilot Chat; grounding and generation vary by license and tenant.

What the trainee gains

How to summarize for an audience, structure a narrative, and create a reviewable slide draft.

Good result

A five-slide draft with a clear message, evidence, risks, and decisions traceable to the report.

Human check: Verify all figures, dates, and conclusions in the source; remove unsupported claims.

Repeated signal

Prepare for a customer meeting when you are unsure what to say

Situation: Your customer call starts in twenty minutes. You know there has been a delivery delay, but your notes are technical, your English draft sounds abrupt, and you are not sure how to explain the impact without promising a date that has not been confirmed. You want a clear opening, a few useful questions, and language you can stand behind.

Back to N9

Without AI

  1. Reread project notes and old customer emails for context.
  2. Write an opening, translate or revise it, then think of questions to ask.
  3. Check dates and commitments before joining the call.

Pain: Preparation is rushed, and it is easy to sound vague or overpromise.

With Microsoft Copilot

  1. Provide approved meeting notes and the confirmed facts in Copilot Chat or Word.
  2. Ask for a short opening, three discovery questions, and likely follow-ups; tell it not to invent dates or commitments.
  3. Revise for your voice, practice the opening, and keep the verified facts nearby.

Tool: Copilot Chat, Word, or Outlook if enabled; use only approved customer information.

What the trainee gains

How to prepare a customer-facing message, ask useful questions, and use AI for language support without surrendering their own judgment.

Good result

A clear meeting opener, three relevant questions, and a short list of verified facts and unresolved items.

Human check: Confirm names, dates, and commitments. Copilot can help you prepare, but you own what you say to the customer.
